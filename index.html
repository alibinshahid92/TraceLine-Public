The problem

Every shop has a version of this

"Which one's current?"

Drawings scattered across shared drives, email threads, and someone's desktop. No single place that says which revision is actually released.

"Who approved this?"

Revisions get emailed around and printed with no enforced sign-off — and no record of who released what, or when.

"Is this the old one?"

Superseded and obsolete drawings keep circulating on the shop floor with nothing on the page to flag that they've been replaced.

AS9100 · Clause 8.5.2 — Control of Documented Information

Built around the clause, not retrofitted to it

§8.5.2 spells out exactly what document control has to do. Traceline's workflow maps to it directly — not as a marketing claim, but as the actual mechanics of the app.

✓
REQUIREMENT

Unique identification & revision status

Every drawing carries a part number and revision letter. Status — draft, pending approval, released, superseded, obsolete — is always shown, never inferred from a filename.

✓
REQUIREMENT

Approval prior to release

A revision can't become "released" without passing Submit → Approve. Enforced at the database level: only one released revision can exist per document, ever.

✓
REQUIREMENT

Current version available at the point of use

One search finds the released drawing — full-text, across part numbers, descriptions, and the text inside the PDF itself. Never a stale copy by accident.

✓
REQUIREMENT

Control of changes & retention of records

Every state change is written to an append-only audit log. Nothing can be edited or deleted after the fact — not even by an admin account. The database itself refuses the write.

✓
REQUIREMENT

Control of obsolete documents

Superseded and obsolete drawings are watermarked the moment they're downloaded — "SUPERSEDED" or "OBSOLETE — DO NOT USE" — so a printed copy can't be mistaken for current.

Traceline is built to support the document-control practices AS9100 §8.5.2 requires. Certification applies to your organization's quality management system as a whole, following an accredited third-party audit — Traceline is the tool, not the certificate.

How it works

The same four steps, every time

01

Upload

Attach the PDF and describe what changed. It lands as a draft only you and reviewers can see.

02

Submit for approval

One click sends it to an Approver. Nothing reaches the shop floor without it.

03

Approve & release

The Approver reviews and releases it. The previous revision is automatically marked superseded.

04

Superseded, automatically

Anyone downloading the old revision from then on gets it clearly watermarked.

What's actually different

Visual diff between revisions

Traceline renders both revisions and highlights exactly what moved, was added, or was removed — no need to eyeball two PDFs side by side.

Full-text search

Search by part number, description, or text pulled from the released drawing itself — works with any CAD-exported PDF with a real text layer.

Role-based access

Viewer, Engineer, Approver, Admin. Engineers upload; only Approvers can release.

Organize by project

Group drawings into projects and filter the dashboard by them — so the list stays navigable as it grows into the thousands.

Audit-trail export

Every revision's full approval history — who, when, what changed — as a PDF you can hand an external auditor without giving them a login.
